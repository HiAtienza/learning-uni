# Empirical Development and Scrum

[[10_UNIVERSITY_CS/Software Engineering 1/Tutor Mode/00 Start Here|Tutor start]] · [[10_UNIVERSITY_CS/Software Engineering 1/Tutor Mode/Unit 03 - Empirical Development and Scrum/01 Tutor Notes|Tutor notes]] · [[10_UNIVERSITY_CS/Software Engineering 1/Tutor Mode/Unit 03 - Empirical Development and Scrum/02 Recall and Exam Practice|Recall and practice]] · [[10_UNIVERSITY_CS/Software Engineering 1/Unit 03 - Empirical Development and Scrum/01 Notes|Standard reference]] · [[10_UNIVERSITY_CS/Software Engineering 1/Unit 03 - Empirical Development and Scrum/03 Lab|Original practical lab]]

## In 60 Seconds

When requirements and technical assumptions are uncertain, progress depends on learning from evidence. Scrum connects three pillars—transparency, inspection and adaptation—to accountabilities, events and artifacts. Acceptance criteria describe what an item must do; Definition of Done establishes the shared quality threshold. Relative estimates support discussion, and observed Done work supports a conditional forecast.

You should be able to locate who owns a decision, explain why “almost done” cannot count as Done, resolve divergent estimates by uncovering assumptions, and calculate a forecast with units and limitations. The Campus Express activity is a UI-refinement exercise; its proposed estimates are not measured team performance. Source: S07 pp.4–46, S09 pp.1–15, S03 pp.1–4 and S14 Q1–Q7.

## 1. Why is learning part of the process?

**Intuition.** If the team learns that “payment” means a live gateway rather than a static selector, it is now estimating a different problem. Ignoring that discovery preserves the old number, not the old validity.

A **defined process** assumes a sufficiently understood and repeatable transformation under controlled conditions. An **empirical process** uses observation and adaptation when significant aspects are uncertain. Software development can reveal needs through use and constraints through implementation. A repeatable build may still be a defined activity within an empirical product effort; these are not labels dividing all work into predictable physical systems and unpredictable software. [^foundations]

**Read the uncertainty mechanism.** The source cone shows an illustrative range of 0.25×–4× at concept stage, narrowing toward 1× at release. An initial reference of 100 would illustrate 25–400, not a guaranteed confidence interval. The diagram's “variance” is estimation spread, not a computed statistical variance. Clarifying scope and testing technical assumptions can improve knowledge; elapsed time alone cannot. [^foundations]

**Worked risk trace.** Two components implement different meanings of “paid.” If the mismatch is found only after many dependent features exist, correction affects design, code, tests and documents. Short integrated increments can expose it earlier. This is a conditional risk-reduction mechanism, not proof that agile eliminates risk or that Waterfall lacks early test planning.

Agile values prefer interaction, working results, collaboration and adaptation when these compete with administrative mechanisms; processes/tools, documentation, contracts and plans still matter. The twelve source principles reinforce the same logic: early valuable delivery, welcoming change, frequent delivery, business–developer cooperation, supported motivated people, direct communication, working-software evidence, sustainable pace, technical excellence, simplicity, self-organization and regular reflection. A useful explanation connects them: feedback only helps if people can communicate and act, while sound design and sustainable work make continuing adaptation feasible. [^agile]

## 2. What turns meetings into empirical control?

**Problem first.** A team holds every scheduled meeting but hides broken integration behind “90% complete.” Why does the meeting schedule fail to make its forecast trustworthy?

**Transparency** makes relevant work and quality understandable. **Inspection** compares that evidence with goals and standards. **Adaptation** changes the work or process when the comparison reveals a meaningful issue. Misleading completion data undermines inspection; inspection without an actionable change does not realize adaptation. [^pillars]

![[10_UNIVERSITY_CS/Software Engineering 1/Unit 03 - Empirical Development and Scrum/Attachments/empirical-learning.svg]]

**Figure 1 — The evidence must survive the whole loop**

**Read it:** Start with understandable work, inspect it against a goal and adjust. Repeat with the changed work made visible. These are linked responsibilities, not one-time Waterfall phases.

**Key takeaway:** A meeting is useful when it improves evidence or a decision, not merely because it occurred.

**Provenance:** Unchanged standard teaching SVG based on S07 p.17 and S09 p.6; informal empirical-learning diagram.

### Locate the decision

| Accountability | What it contributes | Case consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Product Owner | Product value and understandable, ordered backlog | Clarify whether checkout requires real processing or mock selection |
| Developers | Plan and perform work toward a usable Increment, maintaining quality | Explain implementation assumptions, dependencies and estimates |
| Scrum Master | Effective Scrum through coaching, facilitation and impediment removal | Help the team resolve blocked communication; do not assign every task as a taskmaster |

Cross-functional means the team collectively has the required skills, not that every member has every skill. Shared accountability does not erase personal responsibility. A PO who dictates implementation and a Scrum Master who becomes a status clerk each blur a useful distinction. [^scrum]

### Match event to evidence

Sprint Planning establishes the Sprint's purpose, selected work and delivery plan. The Daily Scrum helps Developers inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan. Review examines the product outcome and changed context with stakeholders. Retrospective examines how the team worked and chooses useful improvements. For a one-month Sprint, the source maxima are 8 hours, 15 minutes, 4 hours and 3 hours respectively; the longer events are usually shorter for shorter Sprints. The Daily remains 15 minutes. [^scrum]

**Visible worked distinction.** If customers now need another delivery location, discuss its product implications in ongoing PO collaboration and product review. If broken screen transitions repeatedly escape review, use team reflection to change the checking process—for example, explicitly checking upstream/downstream navigation before sign-off. “Communicate better” lacks an observable action.

The **Product Backlog** is ordered, evolving product work. **Refinement** adds understanding, size and order. The **Sprint Backlog** holds selected work and the practical plan toward the Sprint Goal. An **Increment** must be usable and integrated with earlier work, not merely a collection of disconnected finished parts. [^scrum]

## 3. What counts as Done in this practical?

**Problem first.** The cart displays the correct total, but Checkout navigation is broken and no peer has reviewed it. Does the visible total settle completion?

**Acceptance criteria** are item-specific conditions on behavior. **Definition of Done** is a shared quality standard for completed work. For this particular activity, all four conditions are required: an interactive UI wireframe; fulfilled AC; review by at least one other Developer; verified upstream/downstream screen integration. A static drawing alone does not satisfy interactive flow. The source reading's illustrative code-coverage threshold is not an added Campus Express requirement. [^done]

**Worked decision:** identify the missing evidence—navigation and review. Compare it with the activity DoD. Do not count the item as Done or include partial points in velocity. Repair, review and integrate it, then reassess. Unfinished work returns to the Product Backlog for future consideration; it is not automatically guaranteed the next Sprint. If the screen already satisfies one AC, preserve that fact without confusing it with overall completion.

> [!question] Explain it yourself
> Can an item pass the shared quality checks while implementing the wrong behavior?

> [!success]- Check your explanation
> Yes. Review and integration do not establish that the item satisfies its intended acceptance criteria. Check both the specific behavior and shared quality conditions. Source: S03 p.1; S09 p.11.

## 4. How should the team handle 2 versus 13?

**Intuition.** The votes may represent different hidden assumptions, not simply different confidence levels. Averaging different scopes produces a number with no shared meaning.

The activity's points compare complexity, effort and risk/uncertainty against a reference such as US-01 or US-03 at 2 or 3 points. The required scale is **1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20, 40, 100**. Points are not fixed hours and are not comparable across unrelated teams. [^sizing]

**Worked poker sequence:** explain the item; clarify AC and DoD; each estimator selects privately; reveal together; ask the high and low voters what they included; clarify with the PO; re-vote. The 13 voter may include a real gateway while the 2 voter assumes mock selection. Neither a mean of 5.25 nor rounding it to a legal card resolves that disagreement. No unique final estimate follows from the votes alone.

If the clarified story remains large, split it into meaningful smaller outcomes. The activity requires four fields for every US-01–US-10: PO questions, initial estimate, refined estimate/splits and suggested priority. Order must respect dependencies such as Menu Selection before Cart Summary; recommend value trade-offs to the PO rather than claiming your proposed order is official. The complete original practice is linked in this unit's navigation. [^activity]

No real PO answers, final votes, wireframes or peer checks are supplied by a worked example. If scope changes from mock UI to live integration, revisit estimates and split definitions; do not preserve their numbers as measured facts.

## 5. What does velocity allow you to conclude?

**Problem first.** The tutorial records 18, 22 and 20 points across three Sprints. Can these numbers fix the release date of another team?

A Sprint's **velocity** is the sum of points for items actually meeting DoD in that Sprint. An unfinished 8-point item contributes zero in this convention; count it once when Done. For N observed Sprints, mean velocity is total Done points divided by N. Remaining points divided by this rate gives a **conditional forecast in Sprints**. [^forecast]

**Work the tutorial dataset:**

1. Total observed delivery: 18 + 22 + 20 = 60 points.
2. Mean rate: 60 points ÷ 3 Sprints = **20 points/Sprint**.
3. Remaining duration: 100 points ÷ 20 points/Sprint = **5 additional Sprints**.
4. Calendar conversion: 5 × 2 weeks = **10 weeks**, under the supplied Sprint length.

The arithmetic is exact for these inputs; the forecast is uncertain because future capacity, scope, sizing and quality conditions may change. It is not measured Campus Express velocity. The reading's 150 ÷ 25 = 6 example supplies no Sprint length, so it does not independently establish weeks. [^forecast]

If division produces a fraction and planning requires complete Sprint slots, round upward and say why. If mean velocity is zero, the division gives no usable completion estimate. A three-to-five-Sprint history is the source heuristic, not proof of stability. A team can inflate point values without delivering more value; velocity is not employee productivity or a valid cross-team league table.

### Read the chart’s units before its story

S07 p.46 has **one chart with two vertical axes**: remaining effort in hours on the left, and remaining/completed tasks on the right. The remaining-effort curve, remaining-task curve and completed-task bars must use their own axes. No plotted series supplies Done story-point velocity. The ideal line is a reference trajectory, not a guarantee of uniform work. A line above it calls for investigation; its shape alone does not distinguish scope growth from impediments. [^chart]

### Problem E1 — Defend an agile choice without slogans

**SUPPLEMENTARY PRACTICE.** Synthetic scenario; principles from `SE Methodologies  Scrum - Reading Material.pdf`, PDF pp.1–6, and `Week2-Lecture-Software Processes-r.pdf`, PDF pp.15–37.

A small service has uncertain interaction requirements, can obtain weekly stakeholder feedback, and may deliver useful internal prototypes. Its audit rules must still be documented and reviewed before operational release. Explain whether agile mechanisms are useful, identify one plan-driven control worth retaining, and state one condition that would weaken your recommendation.

> [!tip]- Hint
> Connect each condition to a mechanism; do not answer with “agile is flexible.”

> [!success]- Solution
> Short feedback cycles and transparent increments are useful because the interaction requirements are uncertain and stakeholders can inspect evidence weekly. Internal prototypes allow learning without claiming operational completeness. Retain an explicit reviewed baseline and traceable evidence for the audit rules, because their approval and verification should not disappear inside informal iteration. The recommendation weakens if stakeholders cannot provide timely decisions, if no useful partial outcome can be inspected, or if release requires a fully integrated verified system before any operational use. The justified answer combines learning and control; it does not declare one model universally superior.
>
> Source: course principles in the two cited sources; scenario and synthesis are pedagogical.

### Problem O1 — T7 — Forecast from observed delivery

**ORIGINAL COURSE EXERCISE** · **Difficulty:** ★☆☆

**Source:** Week 3 - Tutorial Questions.docx, question 7.

A Scrum team has completed 18, 22, and 20 story points across three 2-week Sprints.

(a) Calculate the team's mean velocity Ṽ.

(b) If the remaining Product Backlog contains 100 total story points, forecast how many additional Sprints will be required to release the product.

> [!tip]- Hint
> Add completed points, divide by observed Sprints, then divide remaining points by that rate. Keep units.

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** Ṽ = (18 + 22 + 20)/3 = 60/3 = **20 points/Sprint**. **(b)** 100 points ÷ 20 points/Sprint = **5 additional Sprints**, equivalent to **10 weeks** at two weeks each. No rounding is needed. This forecast assumes comparable team capacity, sizing, scope and DoD and that the observed points really were Done. It is not a guaranteed release date, and it cannot be transferred to Campus Express as a measured velocity.

### Problem D1 — Read a dashboard without changing units

**SUPPLEMENTARY PRACTICE.** Synthetic scenario; principles from `SE Methodologies  Scrum - Lecture Slides.pdf`, PDF pp.43–46.

A task-count series drops from 12 to 9 while an effort-hours series drops from 40 to 24. May the team report 19 Done story points by adding the decreases? Explain what can actually be concluded.

> [!tip]- Hint
> Subtract within each unit, then ask whether either quantity counts Done points.

> [!success]- Solution
> The task-count decrease is 3 tasks; the estimated remaining-effort decrease is 16 hours. Adding different units is meaningless, and neither series establishes point velocity. Inspect whether scope or estimates changed and separately sum points for items meeting DoD. Slide 46 likewise combines hours and tasks on separate axes in one chart.

### Problem T1 — When a reasonable forecast stops transferring

**SUPPLEMENTARY PRACTICE.** Synthetic scenario; principles from `SE Methodologies  Scrum - Reading Material.pdf`, PDF pp.12–14; S03 p.4 checkout-scope question.

A team has mean velocity 8 points/Sprint and 41 points left. It now adds live payment processing to work previously scoped as mock UI. Compute the unchanged-input whole-Sprint forecast, then explain why it is insufficient after the scope decision.

> [!tip]- Hint
> Separate correct arithmetic from whether the inputs still describe the new work.

> [!success]- Solution
> For unchanged inputs, 41/8 = 5.125 Sprints, so reserve **6 whole Sprint slots** if planning in whole Sprints. The scope change may alter remaining points, technical uncertainty and relevant historical comparability. Clarify the new outcome, re-estimate affected items and inspect capacity/dependencies before issuing a revised conditional forecast. Keeping the old ratio would preserve arithmetic while ignoring changed meaning. No calendar duration is inferable without Sprint length.
