# CNF, CFL Pumping and Closure

## In 60 Seconds

A CFG generates a language; Chomsky normal form (CNF) reorganises its rules without changing that language. A sufficiently tall binary parse tree repeats a variable on a single root-to-leaf path. Reusing that nested derivation pumps two string pieces together. The CFL pumping lemma gives a necessary condition: break it for every permitted decomposition to prove that a language is not context-free. Closure arguments can then transfer that impossibility to another language.

## Recognise Chomsky Normal Form

Every rule has the form $A\to BC$ or $A\to a$: one variable expands to two variables or to one terminal. If the language contains $\varepsilon$, a fresh start variable may additionally have $S_0\to\varepsilon$ and must never occur on a right-hand side. A unit rule $A\to B$, a mixed rule $A\to aB$, and a three-variable rule $A\to BCD$ are not CNF. $A\to BB$ is allowed: the two children need not have different variable names.

A binary parse tree with a long terminal yield needs a long path. Since there are only finitely many variables, a long enough path repeats one. Two equal variables on separate branches do not give the nested derivation needed for pumping.

**Source:** Lecture 6 pp.8–13, 21–23; Tutorial 6 p.1 and Problem 1. The slide's informal explanation of the short window is not a proof of its bound; use the lemma's stated conditions.

## Convert a CFG Without Changing Its Language

Use this order, keeping track of the original start and nullable variables:

1. Introduce a fresh start $S_0\to S$ that never occurs on a right-hand side.
2. Compute all nullable variables. For each RHS, add the alternatives formed by omitting nullable occurrences; remove epsilon rules except the permitted fresh-start rule. Preserve the empty word when it belongs to the original language.
3. Remove unit rules by copying reachable non-unit productions through unit chains.
4. Replace terminals in RHSs of length at least two by fresh variables such as $U_a\to a$.
5. Split RHSs of length at least three into binary rules using fresh variables. A two-variable RHS already has the required form.

For Tutorial 6 Problem 1, $S\to ABC$, $A\to01$, $B\to\varepsilon$, $C\to11$ generates only 0111. Since B contributes only epsilon, the useful RHS is AC. One CNF is $S_0\to AC$, $A\to U_0U_1$, $C\to U_1U_1$, $U_0\to0$, $U_1\to1$. Verify $S_0\Rightarrow AC\Rightarrow U_0U_1U_1U_1\Rightarrow^*0111$. Deleting a nullable variable outright is valid in this particular grammar because B generates nothing else; in general, preserve both omitted and non-omitted alternatives.

**Source:** Lecture 6 pp.14–20; Tutorial 6 p.2, Problem 1. The precise handling of nullable alternatives and unit chains makes the conversion procedure explicit.

## Read the Two-Piece Pumping Protocol

If L is context-free, there exists $p\ge1$ such that every $s\in L$ with $|s|\ge p$ has a decomposition $s=uvxyz$ satisfying

$$|vy|>0,\qquad |vxy|\le p,\qquad uv^ixy^iz\in L\quad\text{for every }i\ge0.$$

The middle x stays once. The pieces v and y use the same exponent; either one may be empty, but not both. The window bound covers v, x and y together, not just the deleted pieces.

To refute context-freeness, assume L is a CFL; accept the lemma's p; choose a witness depending on p; consider an arbitrary valid decomposition; choose an exponent that breaks it. Your argument must cover **every** valid decomposition. A single successful decomposition does not prove L is context-free, and one bad decomposition does not refute it. If even one decomposition pumps for every exponent, that witness cannot support a contradiction. One pumping success at one exponent proves even less.

**Source:** Lecture 6 pp.22–23; Tutorial 6 pp.1–3, Problem 2. Tutorial 6's final wording in Problem 2 is misleading if read as a fully successful decomposition: such a decomposition defeats that witness, while a single successful exponent does not.

## Prove Three Equal Blocks Are Not Context-Free

Let $L=\{a^nb^nc^n:n\ge0\}$. Assume L is a CFL with pumping length p and choose $s=a^pb^pc^p$. For every valid split, $|vxy|\le p$ prevents the window from meeting both the a and c blocks: it would have to contain all p b's and at least one symbol on each side, requiring at least p+2 symbols.

Thus v and y affect at most two adjacent blocks. Pump down with i=0. Because $|vy|>0$, some count decreases, while at least one of the three counts stays p. Deletion preserves the a-then-b-then-c order, but destroys equal counts. Hence $uxz\notin L$ for every valid split, contradicting the lemma. This proves non-context-freeness, and therefore also nonregularity. Merely proving nonregularity would not have proved non-context-freeness.

**Source:** Lecture 6 pp.24–26 (0/1/2 notation); Tutorial 6 p.3, Problem 3 (a/b/c notation).

## Use Closure With the Correct Language Class

| Operation | Regular languages | Context-free languages |
|---|---|---|
| Union, concatenation, star | Closed | Closed |
| Arbitrary intersection | Closed | Not closed in general |
| Complement relative to a fixed alphabet | Closed | Not closed in general |
| Intersection with a regular language | Closed | Closed |
| Homomorphism | Closed | Closed |

For $A=\{a^mb^nc^n:m,n\ge0\}$ use $S\to DE$, $D\to aD\mid\varepsilon$, $E\to bEc\mid\varepsilon$. For $C=\{a^nb^nc^m:m,n\ge0\}$ use $S\to FG$, $F\to aFb\mid\varepsilon$, $G\to cG\mid\varepsilon$. Their intersection forces all three counts equal, giving the non-CFL above. Therefore CFLs are not closed under arbitrary intersection.

If CFLs were closed under complement, union closure and De Morgan's law would make $A\cap C=\overline{\overline A\cup\overline C}$ context-free, a contradiction. All complements must use the same alphabet. “Not closed” means a counterexample exists; it does not mean every intersection or complement is non-CFL.

**Source:** Lecture 6 pp.27–30; Tutorial 6 pp.2–4, Problem 4. Local clarification: the supplied Problem 4 solution refers to a grammar for C without actually supplying it there; the explicit C construction above fills that omission and is independently checked by its derivation invariant.

## Filter the Copy Language

Tutorial 6 Problem 5 uses $D=\{ww:w\in\{0,1\}^*\}$ and the regular filter $R=0^*1^*0^*1^*$. Their intersection is $H=\{0^m1^n0^m1^n:m,n\ge0\}$. When w contains both symbols, a repeated w matching R must start with 0, end with 1 and have no internal 1-to-0 transition; otherwise ww has too many runs or the wrong endpoints. Thus w is $0^m1^n$. Unary and empty w give the cases m=0 or n=0.

Assume H is context-free and choose $s=0^p1^p0^p1^p$. A window of length at most p meets at most two adjacent blocks, so it cannot touch both members of either matched pair (blocks 1/3 or 2/4). Pump down. If all blocks survive, a changed block has an unchanged partner, breaking equality. If a full block disappears, it uses all p permitted deletions; all other blocks remain. The possible results are $1^p0^p1^p$, $0^{2p}1^p$, $0^p1^{2p}$ or $0^p1^p0^p$. Each contains both symbols but fewer than four maximal blocks, unlike any member of H containing both symbols. Every split fails, so H is not context-free.

If D were context-free, closure under intersection with regular R would make H context-free. Therefore D is not context-free. This uses intersection with a **regular** language, not the false general intersection closure.

**Source:** Tutorial 6 pp.4–6, Problem 5. The run-count argument above expands the supplied filter explanation.

## Tutorial problems

### Problem 1 — Convert the supplied grammar to CNF

Convert $S\to ABC$, $A\to01$, $B\to\varepsilon$, $C\to11$ to CNF and verify its exact language. Source: Tutorial 6 p.2, Problem 1.

> [!tip]- Hint
> Which variable contributes only the empty word?

> [!success]- Solution
> Use $S_0\to AC$, $A\to U_0U_1$, $C\to U_1U_1$, $U_0\to0$, $U_1\to1$. Every rule is binary-variable or single-terminal; S0 never occurs on an RHS. Every complete derivation yields 0111, and 0111 is derivable. No epsilon start rule is needed because the original language excludes epsilon.

### Problem 2 — Control all four pumping choices

Order p, witness, decomposition and exponent; identify who chooses each. Can v and y be pumped independently? Explain what a single successful exponent and a fully pumpable split each establish. Source: Tutorial 6 pp.2–3, Problem 2.

> [!tip]- Hint
> Write the quantifiers before choosing a witness.

> [!success]- Solution
> The assumed CFL supplies p. The prover chooses a word in L of length at least p. An adversary may choose any valid split; the prover chooses an exponent after seeing it. Both v and y use the same exponent. A contradiction handles every split, each with some failing exponent. One successful exponent is insufficient to prove all exponents work; a split that works for every exponent defeats this witness. A single witness cannot establish that the whole language is CFL.

### Problem 3 — Refute three equal blocks

Prove $\{a^nb^nc^n:n\ge0\}$ is not CFL, covering every split. Source: Tutorial 6 p.3, Problem 3.

> [!tip]- Hint
> Use a length-p block for each symbol and pump down.

> [!success]- Solution
> Assume CFL and choose $a^pb^pc^p$. A valid window of length at most p cannot meet both a and c: reaching them requires at least p+2 symbols. It changes at most two adjacent blocks. Pumping down deletes at least one symbol, while one count remains p. Counts cannot all remain equal, so every valid split produces a word outside L at i=0, contradicting the lemma.

### Problem 4 — Disprove two closure claims

Give CFGs for $A=\{a^mb^nc^n:m,n\ge0\}$ and $C=\{a^nb^nc^m:m,n\ge0\}$. Compute their intersection; use it and De Morgan to refute general intersection and complement closure. Source: Tutorial 6 pp.3–4, Problem 4.

> [!tip]- Hint
> Use one component for the equal pair and another for the independent block.

> [!success]- Solution
> For A use $S\to DE$, $D\to aD\mid\varepsilon$, $E\to bEc\mid\varepsilon$; for C use $S\to FG$, $F\to aFb\mid\varepsilon$, $G\to cG\mid\varepsilon$. These produce exactly their stated counts and order. Intersection forces a=b=c, giving the non-CFL of Problem 3. If complements were always CFL, union closure would make $\overline{\overline A\cup\overline C}=A\cap C$ CFL, contradiction. The C grammar is a local completion of an omission in the supplied solution, not a quoted official grammar.

### Problem 5 — Filter the copy language

For $D=\{ww:w\in\{0,1\}^*\}$ and regular $R=0^*1^*0^*1^*$, compute H=D∩R, prove H is not CFL, and conclude D is not CFL. Include complete-block deletion. Source: Tutorial 6 pp.4–6, Problem 5.

> [!tip]- Hint
> Use four length-p blocks, pair 1 with 3 and 2 with 4, and distinguish surviving from deleted blocks.

> [!success]- Solution
> $H=\{0^m1^n0^m1^n:m,n\ge0\}$. In a copied word matching R, a mixed-symbol half must have one 0 run then one 1 run; unary/empty halves give zero exponents. Assume H is CFL and use $0^p1^p0^p1^p$. The short window affects at most two adjacent blocks, never both members of a matched pair. Pump down. If every block survives, a shortened block disagrees with its untouched partner. If a full block disappears, all p deletions are spent there. Results are $1^p0^p1^p$, $0^{2p}1^p$, $0^p1^{2p}$ or $0^p1^p0^p$, each mixed-symbol with fewer than four runs, hence outside H. Thus every split fails. If D were CFL, its intersection with regular R would be CFL; contradiction.
